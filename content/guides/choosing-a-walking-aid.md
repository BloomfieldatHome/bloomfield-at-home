The wrong walking aid can make falls more likely, not less. A quick assessment from a physiotherapist or occupational therapist is worth asking for - ask the GP, or the council if someone is struggling at home.

## Walking sticks

Best for people who are fairly steady but want a bit of support or confidence. The handle should sit at wrist height when the arm hangs down. Held in the hand opposite the weaker leg.

## Walking frames

A frame (sometimes called a Zimmer frame) gives much more support. Frames without wheels are the most stable but slower. Two-wheeled frames are easier to move but still stable. They are mainly for indoors.

## Rollators

A frame with three or four wheels, brakes and often a seat and basket. Great for getting out to the shops and resting along the way. They need good hand strength to work the brakes, and can roll away if the person leans heavily on them - so they are not right for everyone.

## Wheelchairs and scooters

- **NHS wheelchair service:** if someone needs a wheelchair long-term, the GP or a therapist can refer them for an assessment. The service can provide a chair free, or a voucher towards one.
- **Lightweight folding wheelchairs:** useful to keep in the car for days out.
- **Mobility scooters and powerchairs:** good for independence outdoors. Check the person can see, steer and judge traffic safely, and that there is somewhere to store and charge it.

## Before you buy

- Try before you buy where possible - many mobility shops will let you.
- Check the weight limit and the folded size for the car boot.
- Measure doorways at home if it will be used indoors.
- Most mobility aids bought for a disabled person's own use can be sold without VAT - the seller will ask you to sign a simple declaration.
