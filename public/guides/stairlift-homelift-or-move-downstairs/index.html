Stairlift, homelift or living downstairs - how to decide

Updated 21 November 2026 · General information, not professional advice

When stairs become difficult, there are three main options. The right one depends on the person's health, the shape of the house, and how their needs are likely to change.

Option 1: a stairlift

A seat that runs on a rail fixed to the stairs.

  • Good for: people who can transfer on and off a seat safely.
  • Watch out for: curved stairs need a made-to-measure rail, which costs much more and is harder to resell. The person still needs to walk at the top and bottom.
  • Tip: reconditioned and rental stairlifts exist for straight stairs, and can make sense if needs may change quickly.

Option 2: a through-floor homelift

A small lift that travels between floors, usually from a living room into a bedroom.

  • Good for: wheelchair users, people who cannot use a stairlift safely, and couples who both need help.
  • Watch out for: it takes up floor space on both levels and needs a survey. Installation is more involved than a stairlift.

Option 3: living downstairs

Moving the bedroom downstairs, sometimes with a downstairs toilet or shower added.

  • Good for: people whose mobility is declining quickly, or homes where a lift won't fit.
  • Watch out for: losing a living room can make a home feel smaller, and adding a bathroom needs building work.

Get advice before you buy

Ask the council for an occupational therapist assessment first. The OT will look at what is safest now and in the next few years - which avoids paying for a stairlift that stops being usable in a year.

Help with the cost

A Disabled Facilities Grant from the local council can pay for adaptations including stairlifts and lifts. In England the maximum is £30,000 per application, and it is means-tested for adults (Wales up to £36,000, Northern Ireland up to £25,000; Scotland has separate support). Applications go through the council, usually after an OT assessment.

Buying privately

Get at least two written quotes, ask what the servicing and repair costs are after the warranty ends, and never sign on the day of a sales visit if you feel rushed. You have cancellation rights for contracts signed at home, so ask the company to explain them.
