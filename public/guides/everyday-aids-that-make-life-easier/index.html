15 everyday aids that make life at home easier

Updated 21 November 2026 · General information, not professional advice

Many of the things that make daily life harder - opening jars, getting out of a low chair, reaching the back of a cupboard - have simple, cheap solutions. Before buying, ask whether an occupational therapist assessment from the council could provide some of these free.

In the kitchen

  1. Kettle tipper - a cradle that tips the kettle without lifting it.
  2. Jar and bottle openers - grip pads or wall-mounted openers for weaker hands.
  3. Easy-grip cutlery - thicker, cushioned handles.
  4. Perching stool - a tall, slightly sloped stool for resting while cooking or washing up.
  5. Lightweight trolley - for carrying plates and drinks safely while using a walking aid.

In the bathroom

  1. Raised toilet seat and a toilet frame to push up from.
  2. Shower seat or bath board.
  3. Long-handled sponge and toe washer.
  4. Non-slip mats inside and outside the bath.

Around the house

  1. Reacher-grabber - for picking things up without bending.
  2. Chair raisers - lifting a favourite chair to a height that is easier to stand up from.
  3. Big-button phone with photo speed-dial buttons.
  4. Large-print clock showing the day and date - helpful for anyone who loses track of days.
  5. Plug-in motion-sensor night lights for the route to the toilet.
  6. Pill organiser with days of the week - and ask the pharmacist about blister packs if medicines are getting confusing.

Staying connected

Loneliness is as important as physical safety. A simple tablet set up for video calls, a regular phone friendship service from a charity, or a local lunch club can make a big difference.

A word on health products

We do not recommend supplements or health remedies on this site. If someone is concerned about their health, diet or medicines, the GP or pharmacist is the right person to ask.
