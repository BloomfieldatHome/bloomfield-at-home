Personal alarms explained - how they work and how to choose one

Updated 21 November 2026 · General information, not professional advice

A personal alarm is a small button, usually worn as a pendant or on the wrist, that calls for help when pressed. For many families it is the single thing that makes it feel safe for someone to carry on living alone.

How they work

Most alarms have two parts: the button the person wears, and a base unit (or a mobile device) that connects to a response centre. When the button is pressed, a trained operator speaks to the person through a loudspeaker and decides what to do: call a family member or keyholder, or call an ambulance.

The main types

  • Monitored home alarms. A pendant or wristband that works anywhere in the home and garden. The most common choice.
  • Falls detectors. These notice a sudden fall and raise the alarm automatically, which matters if the person is unconscious or too shaken to press anything. They are not perfect, so the button should still be worn.
  • Mobile or GPS alarms. These work outside the home using the mobile network, and can share location. Useful for people who still go out alone.
  • Unmonitored alarms. These call family members directly rather than a response centre. Cheaper, but only works if someone always picks up.

Check the free and local options first

Many councils run their own lifeline or telecare service, sometimes at a low weekly cost, and some provide alarms free after a needs assessment. Ask your council's adult social care team, or search for your council's name plus "lifeline". If the person has carers coming in, ask them what the local service is like.

Questions to ask any provider

  1. Is there a contract, and how much notice do I need to give to cancel?
  2. Is the equipment bought, rented or included in the monthly price?
  3. Who answers the call, and where is the response centre?
  4. Who do you contact if no family member answers? Do you have your own responders?
  5. Does it work without a landline? Many homes no longer have one, so check the base unit uses a mobile signal.
  6. How long does the battery last, and how will we know when it is low?

The bit people forget

An alarm only helps if it is worn. Choose a style the person is happy to wear all day, including in the shower (most pendants are waterproof - check). Test it monthly, and keep a key safe or keyholder arrangement so responders can get in.
