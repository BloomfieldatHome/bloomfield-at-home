Cutting household bills in later life - discounts, social tariffs and free help

Updated 21 November 2026 · General information, not professional advice

A lot of help with bills is available but goes unclaimed, often because people do not know to ask. Most of it is free.

Check benefits first

Pension Credit unlocks a lot of other help, including the Warm Home Discount and broadband social tariffs, yet many eligible pensioners do not claim it. A free benefits check from Age UK, Citizens Advice or an online benefits calculator is the best place to start.

Warm Home Discount

For winter 2026 to 2027, eligible households get £150 off their electricity bill. In England and Wales you usually get it automatically if, on 23 August 2026, you (or your partner) were getting a qualifying means-tested benefit - such as Pension Credit, Universal Credit, Housing Benefit or income-related ESA - and your name was on the electricity bill. Scotland has slightly different rules. Your supplier must be part of the scheme.

Priority Services Register

A free register run by energy and water companies for people who are older, disabled, have a long-term health condition or need extra help. Benefits include advance warning of power cuts, priority support in an emergency, a password scheme to protect against bogus callers, and bills in large print. Ask your energy supplier, electricity network operator and water company to add you.

Broadband and phone social tariffs

Most big providers offer cheaper "social tariffs" for people on Pension Credit, Universal Credit and some other benefits. They can be much cheaper than standard deals and usually have no exit fees. Ask your current provider - or Ofcom's website lists who offers them.

Water bills

Water companies have schemes that cap or reduce bills for low-income households and for people who use a lot of water for medical reasons. Ask your water company about its "social tariff" or "WaterSure" (England and Wales).

Other quick wins

  • Council Tax: a 25% single person discount if someone lives alone, and a reduction or exemption if someone has a severe mental impairment such as dementia.
  • TV Licence: free for over-75s who get Pension Credit.
  • Check you are not paying for things you no longer use - old phone lines, insurance add-ons, subscriptions.
