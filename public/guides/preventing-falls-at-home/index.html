Preventing falls at home - a room-by-room checklist

Updated 21 November 2026 · General information, not professional advice

Falls are one of the most common reasons older people end up in hospital, and many happen at home doing ordinary things. Most of the fixes are cheap and quick.

Start with the free assessment

In England, anyone who is struggling at home can ask their council's adult social care team for a needs assessment, which often includes a visit from an occupational therapist (OT). An OT can recommend - and councils often provide - equipment such as grab rails, raised toilet seats and bath boards. Ask for this before buying expensive equipment. If someone has had a fall, also tell their GP: many areas have a falls prevention service.

Room by room

Hallways and stairs - Good lighting at the top and bottom of the stairs, ideally with switches at both ends. - A second handrail, so there is something to hold on both sides. - No loose rugs, trailing wires or clutter on the stairs.

Living room - Furniture arranged so there is a clear walking route. - A chair with arms and a firm seat that is easy to get out of. - Phone (or alarm) within reach of the usual chair.

Bathroom - Grab rails by the toilet and bath or shower. - A non-slip mat inside and outside the bath. - A shower seat or bath board if standing is tiring.

Bedroom - A bedside lamp or motion-sensor night light for trips to the toilet. - Bed at a height where feet are flat on the floor when sitting.

Kitchen - Everyday items moved to between waist and shoulder height, so there is no climbing or bending. - Spills wiped up straight away.

Footwear and the person themselves

Well-fitting slippers or shoes with a back and a grippy sole make a real difference. Ask the GP to review medicines if the person feels dizzy, and get eyesight checked regularly.

If bigger changes are needed

Ramps, level-access showers and stairlifts may be covered by a Disabled Facilities Grant from the council. In England this can be up to £30,000, depending on income and savings (Wales and Northern Ireland have different limits, and Scotland has its own schemes). See our guide to stairlifts and homelifts.
